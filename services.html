All services are free of charge. No referral needed. Walk in or contact us directly.

Screening and Brief Interventions

A quick process where healthcare workers identify patients who may be at risk of harmful behaviours like substance use or mental health issues, then offer short, focused conversations to motivate behaviour change before problems become serious.

MedicalExaminations and Health Screening

Routine check-ups and tests to assess a person's overall health — things like blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, and physical exams. The goal is early detection of diseases or conditions before symptoms appear.

Counselling Services

Counselling, peer support groups and mental health services to address the emotional and social aspects of substance use.

Needle Exchange Programmes

Free, sterile equipment to reduce the risk of HIV, Hepatitis C and other blood-borne infections for people who inject drugs.

Opioid Substance Therapy

Evidence-based medication-assisted treatment to reduce dependence and support long-term recovery in a safe, non-judgemental environment.

HIV Testing and TB Screening

Specific tests to check whether a person has HIV (the virus that causes AIDS) or TB. Both are highly relevant in South Africa given the high burden of these diseases. Early detection means earlier treatment and reduced spread.

Social Services

Support services that address non-medical needs affecting a person's wellbeing — such as housing, food security, grants, child welfare, and connecting people to community resources. Often provided alongside healthcare to address the broader social determinants of health.

Skill Development

Programmes that equip individuals with practical life or vocational skills — such as job readiness, financial literacy, or coping strategies. In a healthcare setting, this often targets people in recovery or those facing social vulnerabilities.

Post-Rehabilitation Support Services

Care and support provided after a person completes a rehabilitation programme . This includes relapse prevention, counselling, peer support, and follow-up to help maintain progress and prevent setbacks.

COSUP

Community Oriented Substance Use Programme. Serving Tshwane since 2015 across 16 sites. A partnership between the City of Tshwane and the University of Pretoria.
